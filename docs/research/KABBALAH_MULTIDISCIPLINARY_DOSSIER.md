# Research dossier: Kabbalah, occult claims, religious authority, and social power

**Project:** Grey Company Research Lab / Occult Killer corpus  
**Status:** User-provided multidisciplinary research synthesis, augmented with selected verified bibliographic references; **not** a finding that a religion or secret network collectively engages in wrongdoing.  
**Scope:** Comparative theology, history of religion, psychology, neuroscience, sociology, political economy.  
**Model suitability:** Historical context and candidate hypotheses only. No automatic swarm personality presets, motive priors, or criminality scores may be inferred from religious affiliation.

## Evidence model

Every individual claim belongs to one of these strata:

1. **Documented text/doctrine:** A proposition is present in an identifiable source. Existence of a ritual prescription is distinct from evidence that the ritual works.
2. **Documented conduct:** A particular individual's or organization's behavior is supported by independent records, judicial findings, or other reliable evidence.
3. **Interpretive framework:** An ethical or religious analysis of observable conduct, explicitly attributed to its framework.
4. **Unverified allegation:** A specific claim lacking sufficient corroboration; records must preserve original source and independence clusters.
5. **Discredited proposition:** A claim contradicted by reliable evidence or shown to originate in a fabrication.

Never promote an allegation about an individual into a claim about a whole religious, ethnic, or historical group. Identity, social association, coordination, and coordinated wrongdoing require different evidence.

## A. Historical and metaphysical claims

- **Traditions:** Distinguish theoretical, meditative, and practical Kabbalah, and distinguish Jewish Kabbalah from Western Hermetic Qabalah.
- **Jewish mystical concepts:** Sefirot, shefa, Yesod, kelipot, gilgul, ritual intention, sexual mystical symbolism, and the meaning of religious discipline. Treat these as historical theological concepts rather than biomedical measurements.
- **Practical Kabbalah and older Jewish magic:** Some texts describe amulets, names, invocations, divination-like practices, and attempts to affect relationships. These descriptions establish that practices were recorded; they do not establish supernatural efficacy or support allegations of remote neurological control.
- **Historical divergences and authority:** Sabbateanism and Frankism are specific movements requiring their own primary and scholarly sources. Their controversies cannot establish ordinary Kabbalistic practice.
- **Scientific distinction:** The existence of religious metaphysical claims does not establish measurable energy transfers, alternate timelines, or physical control of another person's thoughts.

## B. Psychological and clinical questions

Study ordinary explanatory mechanisms that could make an unverified supernatural threat feel consequential: expectation, threat appraisal, stress, attention, vigilance, persuasion, conformity, and learning.

Religious belief by itself is not a psychiatric diagnosis. Assess distress, impairment, cultural context, corroboration, and alternative explanations separately. For possible **spiritual abuse**, examine concrete controlling acts (isolation, pressure, threats, financial dependency, suppression of dissent), not assumed occult influence.

Do not use an AI simulator to diagnose named individuals or to validate hidden targeting based on ambiguous events.

## C. Social-network and institutional questions

- **Association:** Shared memberships, friendships, theology, and social clubs can transmit ideas and trust.
- **Alignment:** Similar norms can produce similar choices without direct contact or plans.
- **Coordination:** A claim of a joint program requires communications, agreements, transactions, or decisions that support it.
- **Misconduct:** Establish specific acts, rule violations, impacts, and accountable participants independently.

Candidate mechanisms for ethical and unethical network outcomes include: social capital, charismatic authority, information asymmetry, loyalty, normalization of deviance, conflicts of interest, oversight quality, whistleblowing protections, and institutional capture.

Include the null hypothesis: an organization may have **no special interest** in an independent founder or any other person.

## D. Economic questions

Religious instruction, publications, donations, and networking can be legitimate. Risk analysis should concern *observable* variables such as misleading promises, unverifiable paid services, undisclosed conflicts, coercive dependence, fiduciary duties, and actual misuse of funds.

A spiritual service can exhibit **credence-good** characteristics when a purchaser cannot independently assess the promised result. This is a potential market vulnerability, not a blanket description of any particular religion.

## H. Simulation integration rules

1. Textual claims can suggest **hypotheses to test**; they cannot directly alter a real group's default personality or hidden motive.
2. Add fictional scenarios of authority, information asymmetry, group loyalty, misconduct reporting, or external market entry with matched counterfactual conditions.
3. Require independent evidence before comparing a real entity to a synthetic profile.
4. Separate **model utility score**, **synthetic response frequency**, and **empirically calibrated probability**.
5. Preserve counterfactuals where no response or ethical cooperation occurs.
6. For sexual violence and severe criminal misconduct, model abstract incident detection, protection, reporting, accountability and harms; never generate perpetration tactics or sensitive recordings.
7. No neural inference of dopamine receptor function from books, religion, or rumor. Reward sensitivity is an explicit hypothetical model variable.
