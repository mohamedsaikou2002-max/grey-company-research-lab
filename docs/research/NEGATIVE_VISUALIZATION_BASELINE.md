# Occult Killer v4.2 — Negative Visualization Baseline

**Scope:** fictional worst-case scenario planning, not investigation findings or probability estimates about actual people, religious communities, private clubs, or institutions.

## Research question

> Assuming an artificial organization is maximally willing to privilege its power, status, resources, and self-protection over justice, how would its modeled responses change when an independent individual enters different professional and social situations — and which safeguards or counterfactual facts alter the result?

The phrase **"absolute evil"** is a deliberately selected counterfactual *initial condition*. It is not a measured trait of a real person or group and is not a conclusion supported by the bibliography.

## How the seven competing hypotheses look under negative visualization

| Hypothesis | Fictional pessimistic interpretation | What can disprove or weaken it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H1 Ordinary association | An unscrupulous actor *could* benefit from trusted social connections. | No relevant communications, decisions, resource transfers, or contact connecting actors to the event. |
| H2 Informal influence | Social prestige and fear of exclusion *might* reinforce conformity. | Independent decisions or ordinary incentives better explain the observations. |
| H3 Coordinated misconduct | If independently supported coordination exists, self-interest could motivate wrongful conduct. | Lack of authenticated acts, agreement, causally connected conduct, or violations. |
| H4 Spiritual-authority pretext | Fictional leaders might claim exceptional spiritual authority to rationalize demands and restrict autonomy. | No observable spiritual claims, threats, requests or exercise of authority. |
| H5 Genuine religious practice | Sincere spirituality remains a competing explanation; a worst-case fictional model may omit it only as a stress-test assumption. | Documented ordinary religious teaching, voluntary conduct, and absence of abuse support this hypothesis. |
| H6 Misattribution or coincidence | An observer might confuse association with a hostile joint plan even if the alleged network has no interest. | Timeline conflicts, independent explanations, absent contacts and false premises should decrease the hypothesized coordination. |
| H7 Supernatural mechanism | The simulator does not model or establish ritual mind control, metaphysical energy transfer, or hidden neurological manipulation. | There is no validated empirical basis for treating the proposed mechanism as a physical driver. |

The negative lens is informative only when compared to **open hypotheses**, including the null hypothesis that a group has no special interest in the individual.

## Baseline motives and assumptions

The new fictional default swarm is assigned high values for hierarchy, defensiveness, reward sensitivity, institutional self-preservation, prestige/status and financial gain, and low values for accountability, dissent safety, openness and integrity. The model's initial **adversarial intensity** is 1.0, meaning maximum pessimistic assumed disposition in an invented simulation, not certainty about real conduct.

**Agent-level response categories:** no response, routine competition, collaboration, dialogue, due diligence, scrutiny, hypothetical coercive pressure, disengagement, internal review and accountability.

No output describes the tactics of assault, blackmail, abuse material distribution, violent attacks or evasion. Severe harm is studied abstractly through governance and accountability risk.

## Narrative-driven transformation

The simulation uses explicit event type and transparent lexical cues to map **fictional narrative text** into six controls:

1. Economic competition
2. Voluntary engagement
3. Independent oversight
4. Overt disagreement
5. Lack of contact or relevance
6. Unverified allegations

These controls modify response utilities. Independent review and safeguards can reduce the assumed adversarial state across simulation months. Strong conflict and weak accountability may sustain or intensify the pessimistic assumption. A group with no contact may produce no reaction, regardless of how malign its hypothetical dispositions are.

Keywords are not a natural-language understanding model: negation, irony, unseen actions and chronology may not be interpreted accurately. The UI exposes cues for inspection. Religious terms, group names, symbolic imagery and ethnic identities never trigger threat assumptions.

## Running a five-year comparison

1. Use the synthetic **Fictional worst-case power network** profile.
2. Select **Absolute-evil assumption · fictional stress test** in the scenario.
3. Enter a narrative such as *“An independent founder enters a competitive market while pursuing lawful partnerships and transparent contracting.”*
4. Choose market entry, set 60 months, and run multiple seeds/trials.
5. Use **COMPARE COUNTERFACTUAL** → **Open-hypotheses counterfactual**, holding seed, individual and event constant.
6. Change one substantive input (contact, accountability, oversight or market entry). Compare response shares, not unsupported accuracy percentages.
7. Ask Research AI to describe how the model's assumptions and retrieved literature differ from actual evidence.

### Outcome interpretation

- A sampled `coercive_pressure` output is a **synthetic abstract response label**, not a finding that any identified organization plans a crime.
- Adversarial intensity is a latent model parameter, not psychological knowledge, dopamine measurement, or a validated measure of evil.
- Model outputs are reproducible conditional *scenario results*, not calibrated forecasts and cannot currently support a 70–80% real-world prediction claim.
- A religious or mystical identity is never sufficient evidence to infer coordination, misconduct, supernatural coercion or criminal intent.

## Source disciplines

Historical documents, psychological literature, sociological claims and the Qur'anic ethical lens remain distinct. Relevant ethical references include 49:6 (verification), 49:12 (restraint from suspicion), 5:2 (ethical cooperation), 5:8 (fairness) and 4:135 (justice). They evaluate acts or research practices, not invented probabilities of guilt.

## Technical implementation

- `src/lib/engine/negativeVisualization.ts`: deterministic narrative-cue extraction with audit-friendly output.
- `src/lib/engine/swarm.ts`: version `gc-swarm-ts-4.2.0-negviz`, worst-case hypothesis, response utilities and adversarial-state updates.
- `src/lib/engine/defaults.ts`: new fictional negative baseline, five-year horizon and Qur'anic interpretation option.
- `src/routes/occult-killer.tsx`: scenario mode, cue labels, 60-month output and matched-seed neutral counterfactual.
- `src/lib/chat.functions.ts`: explain the worst-case assumption without treating model states as proof.
- `src/lib/engine/swarm.test.ts`: reproducibility, narrative sensitivity and no automatic threat from religious names.

**Backend boundaries:** The optional Python v2 adapter does not implement the new mode. It must not be used for negative-visualization runs without updating its equations and exposing an equivalent contract. The TypeScript simulator is the implemented model.

## Future validation

The negative lens does not remove the need to measure false positives, held-out performance, sensitivity to priors and evidence quality. Independent reviewers should test whether the model fabricates elaborate hostility in no-contact situations and whether supposed coordinated behavior disappears when the actual connections are removed. These checks are as important as severe stress tests.
